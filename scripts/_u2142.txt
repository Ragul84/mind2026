##### DAY 22: UPSC History: Indus Valley Civilization & Vedic Age
  • Indus Valley Civilization (2500–1750 BCE): Urban features: Grid system, burnt bricks, drainage, Citadel. Major Sites : Harappa (Cemetery R37), Mohenjo-daro (Great Bath), Lothal (Dockyard), Dholavira (Water reservoir, 3-tier city), Rakhigarhi (Largest site). Trade : Seals (Steatite), Beads, Lapis Lazuli (from Afghanistan). Script : Boustrophedon (undeciphered). Religion : Mother Goddess, Pashupati Seal, Peepal tree.
  • Early Vedic Period (1500–1000 BCE): Society: Tribal (Jana), Pastoral. Main wealth : Cow (Gau). Political : Sabha & Samiti (assemblies). Religion : Natural forces (Indra-Rain, Agni-Fire, Varuna-Water). No idols or temples. Rig Veda : Oldest, contains Gayatri Mantra.
  • Later Vedic Period (1000–600 BCE): Society: Sedentary agriculture. Rise of Kingdoms (Janapadas). Varna system becomes hereditary. Status of women declines. Rise of rituals/sacrifices. New Gods : Prajapati (Creator), Vishnu, Rudra. Sama, Yajur, Atharva Vedas composed.
  • Archaeological Cultures: IVC: Ochre Coloured Pottery (OCP) in some parts. Early Vedic : Painted Grey Ware (PGW). Later Vedic : Northern Black Polished Ware (NBPW) begins towards the end.
  • Key Terms (Terminology): Gavisthi: Search for cows (War). Duhitri : Daughter (Milker of cow). Bali : Voluntary offering (later compulsory tax). Kula : Family. Gramani : Village head.

##### DAY 23: UPSC History: Mahajanapadas, Buddhism & Jainism
  • The 16 Mahajanapadas: Magadha emerged as the most powerful. Key dynasties : Haryanka (Bimbisara, Ajatashatru), Shishunaga, Nanda (Mahapadma Nanda). Causes of Magadha's rise: Iron deposits, fertile land, elephants in army, strategic capitals (Rajgir, Pataliputra).
  • Buddhism: The Middle Path: Gautama Buddha (Lumbini, 563 BCE). Four Noble Truths. Eightfold Path (Ashtangika Marga). Concept of Nirvana. No God/Soul (Anatta). Councils : 1st (Rajgir-Ajatashatru), 2nd (Vaishali-Kalashoka), 3rd (Pataliputra-Ashoka), 4th (Kashmir-Kanishka).
  • Jainism: Triratna: Vardhamana Mahavira (24th Tirthankara). Five Vows (Ahimsa, Satya, Asteya, Aparigraha, Brahmacharya). Syadvada (theory of relativity). Extreme penance. Sects : Digambara (sky-clad), Shvetambara (white-clad). Councils : 1st (Pataliputra), 2nd (Vallabhi).
  • Key Symbols & Events: Buddha: Birth (Lotus/Bull), Renunciation (Horse), Enlightenment (Bodhi tree), First Sermon (Wheel), Death (Stupa).
  • Art & Culture Link: Stupa architecture (Sanchi), Chaityas (Prayer halls), Viharas (Monasteries). Gandhara, Mathura, and Amravati schools of art.

##### DAY 24: UPSC History: Mauryan Empire & Ashokan Dhamma
  • Mauryan Expansion: Chandragupta Maurya (321 BCE) with Chanakya's help defeated the Nandas and Seleucus Nicator. Bindusara expanded to the south. Ashoka (273–232 BCE) completed the empire by conquering Kalinga (261 BCE).
  • Mauryan Administration: Highly centralized. Saptanga Theory (King, Amatya, Janapada, Durga, Kosha, Danda, Mitra). Important officers : Samaharta (Tax), Sannidhata (Treasury). Spy system (Gudha Purushas).
  • Ashokan Dhamma: Following the Kalinga war, Ashoka replaced Bherighosha (War drum) with Dhammaghosha (Sound of Dhamma). Dhamma principles : Respect to elders, tolerance towards all sects, non-violence, social responsibility. Spread via Dhamma Mahamatras.
  • Edicts & Inscriptions: Major Rock Edicts (14): XIII mentions Kalinga war. Pillar Edicts (7). Languages : Prakrit (mostly), Greek, Aramaic. Scripts : Brahmi (mostly), Kharosthi (NW India). James Prinsep deciphered Brahmi in 1837.
  • Mauryan Art & Architecture: Pillars: Monolithic, polished, animals on top (Sarnath Lion Capital). Stupas : Sanchi Stupa. Caves : Barabar Caves (donated to Ajivikas). First stone architecture on a large scale.

##### DAY 25: UPSC History: Post-Mauryan Period & Kushanas
  • Native Dynasties: Shungas: Pushyamitra Shunga (revived Brahmanism). Satavahanas : Deccan region, led by Gautamiputra Satakarni. They issued Lead coins and started Land Grants to Brahmins. Sangam Dynasties : Cheras, Cholas, Pandyas in the deep South.
  • Foreign Dynasties: Indo-Greeks: Menander (Milinda-panha). Kushanas : Kanishka (78 AD - start of Saka Era). Kanishka's capital: Purushpura (Peshawar). He was a great patron of Buddhism.
  • Mahayana Buddhism: Emerged during this period. Features : Idol worship of Buddha, Bodhisattvas (compassionate beings who delay nirvana), use of Sanskrit instead of Pali. Supported by Kanishka.
  • Schools of Art: Gandhara School: Greek influence (Apollo-like Buddha), bluish-grey schist stone. Mathura School : Indigenous, red sandstone, cheerful Buddha. Amravati School : White marble, focus on narrative scenes (Jataka tales), Satavahana patronage.
  • Economic Links: Rise of the "Silk Route" (Kushanas controlled it). Trade with Rome (spices, silk, gold). Growth of Guilds (Shrenis).

##### DAY 26: UPSC History: The Gupta Empire — The Golden Age
  • Political History: Chandragupta I (319 AD): Started Gupta Era. Samudragupta : Prayag Prashasti (written by Harisena) describes his vast conquests. Chandragupta II : Defeated Sakas, Fa-Hien (Chinese traveler) visited his court.
  • Administration & Economy: Decentralized (unlike Mauryas). Rise of Feudalism (Samantas). High gold coins (Dinars) but lesser in purity than Kushanas. Decline in urban centers towards the end.
  • Science & Literature: Aryabhatta: Aryabhatiyam (Zero, Pi, Earth's rotation). Varahamihira : Brihat Samhita (Astronomy). Kalidasa : Shakuntalam, Meghadutam. Vishnu Sharma : Panchatantra. Vatsyayana : Kama Sutra.
  • Art & Architecture: Birth of Hindu Temple Architecture (Nagara style begins). Dashavatara Temple (Deogarh). Ajanta Caves (Vakataka/Gupta period paintings). Sultanganj Buddha (Copper statue).
  • Religion: Revival of Brahmanism (Bhagavatism). Focus on Bhakti towards Vishnu and Shiva. Final compilation of Puranas and Epics (Ramayana/Mahabharata).

##### DAY 27: UPSC History: South Indian Kingdoms & Sangam Age
  • Sangam Age (Tamil Nadu): Cheras (Kerala), Cholas (Uraiyur), Pandyas (Madurai). Literature : Ettuthokai, Pattuppattu, Silappatikaram. Social : 5 Landscapes (Thinais). Keezhadi excavations proof of urban culture.
  • Pallavas of Kanchi: Greatest: Mahendravarman I, Narasimhavarman I. Architecture : Ratha temples (Mahabalipuram), Shore temple, Kailasanathar temple. They pioneered the Dravidian style.
  • Chalukyas of Badami: Pulakeshin II: Defeated Harshavardhana (Aihole inscription). Architecture : Pattadakal (UNESCO site), Aihole (Cradle of Indian temple architecture). Vesara style (blend of North and South).
  • Rashtrakutas: Greatest: Dantidurga, Amoghavarsha. Architecture : Kailasa Temple at Ellora (Monolithic - carved out of a single rock by Krishna I). This is a masterpiece of world heritage.
  • Key Terms (South): Eripatti: Land for maintenance of village tank. Ghatika : Educational centers (mostly Brahmins). Ur : General village assembly. Sabhai : Brahmin village assembly.

##### DAY 28: UPSC REVISION: Ancient India Finale & Map Drill
  • Ancient India Timeline Recap: 2500-1750 BCE: IVC. 1500-1000 BCE: Early Vedic. 1000-600 BCE: Late Vedic. 600 BCE: Mahajanapadas & Heterodox sects. 322-185 BCE: Mauryas. 200 BCE-300 AD: Post-Mauryan (Kushanas, Satavahanas). 319-550 AD: Guptas. 600-800 AD: Pallavas, Chalukyas.
  • Master Site Drill (Map Memory): Lothal/Dholavira: Gujarat (Maritime/Water). Sarnath : UP (1st Sermon). Rajgir/Pataliputra: Bihar (Capitals). Mahabalipuram : TN (Shore temples). Badami/Pattadakal: KA (Chalukyas). Ellora : MH (Rashtrakutas).
  • Religious Comparison Table: Buddhism: Middle Path, Nirvana, Anatta (No soul). Jainism : Extreme penance, Triratna, Anekantavada. Hinduism : Bhakti, Varna system, Temple architecture evolution.
  • Art & Architecture Anchors: Mauryan: Monolithic pillars. Gandhara : Greek Buddha. Mathura : Red sandstone. Gupta : Nagara temples begin. Pallava : Dravidian Rathas. Rashtrakuta : Monolithic Kailasa temple.
  • Important Travelers: Megasthenes (Mauryas - Greek). Fa-Hien (Guptas - Chinese). Hiuen Tsang (Harsha - Chinese).

##### DAY 29: UPSC History: Delhi Sultanate — The Five Dynasties
  • The Five Dynasties: 1. Slave (Mamluk): Qutb-ud-din Aibak, Iltutmish (real founder), Razia (1st woman ruler). 2. Khalji : Alauddin Khalji (Market reforms, Dagh & Chehra). 3. Tughlaq : Ghiyasuddin, Muhammad bin Tughlaq (Token currency, Capital shift), Firoz Shah (Canals, Jizya on Brahmins). 4. Sayyid. 5. Lodi : Bahlul, Sikandar (Gaj-i-Sikandari), Ibrahim (defeated in Panipat).
  • Administrative Terms: Iqta System: Land grants in lieu of salary (Iltutmish). Diwan-i-Arz: Military dept (Balban). Diwan-i-Kohi: Agriculture dept (M.B. Tughlaq). Diwan-i-Khairat: Charity (Firoz Tughlaq).
  • Market Reforms (Alauddin Khalji): Price control of essential goods. Appointment of Shahna-i-Mandi (market supervisor). It was the most sophisticated economic control system of medieval India.
  • Architecture: Qutub Minar (Aibak/Iltutmish). Alai Darwaza (Alauddin). Tomb of Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq. Use of Arch, Dome, and Minaret. Decoration with floral patterns and calligraphy (Arabesque).
  • Literature: Amir Khusrau (Tut-i-Hind): Invented Sitar, Tabla, and Qawwali. Ziauddin Barani (Tarikh-i-Firoz Shahi). Ibn Battuta (Rihla): Visited during M.B. Tughlaq's reign.

##### DAY 30: UPSC History: Vijayanagara & Bahmani Kingdoms
  • Vijayanagara Empire (1336–1646): Founded by Harihara and Bukka (Sangama dynasty). Four dynasties : Sangama, Saluva, Tuluva, Aravidu. Greatest King : Krishnadeva Raya (Tuluva). Battle of Talikota (1565): Combined Deccan Sultanates defeated Vijayanagara, leading to its decline.
  • Administration: Nayakara System: Similar to Iqta. Military chiefs (Nayakas) were given lands (Amara) for maintenance of troops. They had to pay a portion of revenue to the King and attend the court.
  • Krishnadeva Raya: The Golden Age: "Andhra Bhoja". Authored "Amuktamalyada" (Telugu). Patronized "Ashtadiggajas" (8 great poets, including Tenali Rama). Built Vithalaswami and Hazara Rama temples.
  • Bahmani Sultanate (1347–1527): Founded by Alauddin Bahman Shah (Hasan Gangu). Capital : Gulbarga, then Bidar. Mahmud Gawan (Prime Minister) was the most famous administrator. Later split into 5 Sultanates: Bijapur, Golconda, Ahmednagar, Bidar, Berar.
  • Foreign Travelers to Vijayanagara: Nicolo Conti (Italian), Abdur Razzaq (Persian - "eyes never saw such a city"), Domingo Paes (Portuguese), Fernao Nuniz (Portuguese). Their accounts are the primary source for the city's splendor.

##### DAY 31: UPSC History: The Mughal Empire — Consolidation & Zenith
  • The Great Mughals: Babur: Founded (1526, Panipat I). Humayun : Exiled by Sher Shah Suri (GT Road founder). Akbar : Real consolidator, Mansabdari, Din-i-Ilahi. Jahangir : Chain of Justice, Painting peak. Shah Jahan : Golden Age of Architecture. Aurangzeb : Maximum expansion, religious orthodoxy.
  • Mansabdari System: Rank-based system (Zat and Sawar). Zat : personal rank/salary. Sawar : number of cavalrymen to be maintained. Ranks were not hereditary. It was the backbone of Mughal military-admin.
  • Land Revenue: Zabti & Dahshala: Raja Todar Mal (Akbar's Finance Minister) introduced the Dahshala system. Land was surveyed and tax fixed based on average yield of last 10 years. Categories : Polaj (annual), Parauti (1-2y gap), Chachar (3-4y), Banjar (5y+).
  • Religious Policy: Akbar: Ibadat Khana (House of Worship), Mazhar (Infallibility decree), Sulh-i-Kul. He abolished Jizya and Pilgrimage tax. Aurangzeb : Re-imposed Jizya, destroyed some temples, faced revolts from Marathas, Sikhs, and Rajputs.
  • Mughal Architecture: Features: Pietra Dura (stone inlay), Charbagh (four-square garden), Double Dome. Examples : Taj Mahal, Red Fort, Jama Masjid, Fatehpur Sikri (Buland Darwaza).

##### DAY 32: UPSC History: The Maratha Empire & Shivaji
  • Shivaji Maharaj (1627–1680): Founded the Maratha Kingdom. Famous for guerrilla warfare (Ganimi Kava). Forts : Raigad (Capital), Pratapgad. Coronation in 1674 at Raigad.
  • Ashtapradhan (Council of 8): 1. Peshwa (PM). 2. Amatya (Finance). 3. Mantri (Intelligence). 4. Sachiv (Correspondence). 5. Sumant (Foreign). 6. Senapati (Military). 7. Panditrao (Religious). 8. Nyayadhish (Justice).
  • Maratha Taxation: Chauth: 1/4th of revenue paid by non-Maratha territories to avoid Maratha raids. Sardeshmukhi : Additional 10% tax paid to Shivaji as the hereditary head (Sardeshmukh) of the land.
  • The Peshwas (1713–1818): Power shifted from the Chhatrapati to the Peshwa (Brahmin PMs). Balaji Vishwanath, Baji Rao I (Soldier-Statesman), Balaji Baji Rao. Third Battle of Panipat (1761): Marathas (Sadashiv Rao Bhau) defeated by Ahmad Shah Abdali.
  • Maratha Confederacy: The empire split into 5 centers: Peshwa (Pune), Scindia (Gwalior), Holkar (Indore), Gaekwad (Baroda), Bhonsle (Nagpur).

##### DAY 33: UPSC History: Bhakti & Sufi Movements
  • Sufi Movement: Mystical dimension of Islam. Key concepts : Pir (Teacher), Murid (Disciple), Khanqah (Hospice), Sama (Music). Silsilahs : 1. Chisti (Moinuddin Chisti, Nizamuddin Auliya - most popular, simple life). 2. Suhrawardi (Orthodox, close to state). 3. Naqshbandi (very orthodox).
  • Bhakti Movement (North): Nirguna (God without form): Kabir (Dohas), Guru Nanak (Sikhism founder). Saguna (God with form): Tulsidas (Ramcharitmanas), Surdas, Mirabai (Krishna devotee), Chaitanya Mahaprabhu (Kirtan).
  • Bhakti Movement (South/West): Alvars (Vaishnava) and Nayanmars (Saiva). Shankaracharya (Advaita - Non-dualism). Ramanuja (Vishishtadvaita). Basavanna (Lingayats/Virashaivas - Karnataka). Vithoba cult (Maharashtra - Tukaram, Namdev, Jnaneshwar).
  • Common Features: Discarded rituals and sacrifices. Preached in regional languages (Tamil, Hindi, Marathi, Punjabi). Rejected caste barriers. Emphasized a personal bond with God.
  • Impact on Literature: Growth of regional languages. Kabir's Bijak, Guru Granth Sahib (Sikhism), Ramcharitmanas, Abhangas (Maharashtra).

##### DAY 34: UPSC History: Decline of Mughals & Regional Powers
  • Causes of Mughal Decline: Weak successors. Jagirdari Crisis (shortage of land). Financial bankruptcy due to Aurangzeb's wars. Invasions : Nadir Shah (1739 - took Peacock Throne), Abdali (Panipat III). Rise of autonomous states.
  • Successor States: Hyderabad: Founded by Nizam-ul-Mulk (Asaf Jah) in 1724. Bengal : Murshid Quli Khan. Awadh : Saadat Khan (Burhan-ul-Mulk). These states nominally accepted Mughal authority but were independent.
  • The Sikhs: Transformed into a military brotherhood by Guru Gobind Singh (Khalsa). Rise of Ranjit Singh (Sher-e-Punjab) who unified the Misls and established the Sikh Empire in Punjab.
  • Mysore & Hyder Ali: Rise of Hyder Ali and Tipu Sultan in the South. They modernised the army and posed a serious challenge to the British.
  • The Jats & Rajputs: Jats: Suraj Mal (Plato of the Jats). Rajputs : Amber, Marwar regained independence but faced internal conflicts.

##### DAY 35: UPSC REVISION: Medieval India Finale & Terminology Drill
  • Medieval Timeline Recap: 1206-1526: Delhi Sultanate. 1336-1646: Vijayanagara Empire. 1526-1707: Great Mughals. 1674-1818: Maratha Empire. 1707-1757: Rise of Regional Powers.
  • Administrative Terminology Master List: Iqta: Land grant (Sultanate). Amara : Land grant (Vijayanagara). Mansab : Rank (Mughal). Chauth/Sardeshmukhi: Tax (Maratha). Diwan-i-Arz: Military (Sultanate). Samaharta : Revenue (Maurya - recap). Ashtapradhan : 8 Ministers (Shivaji).
  • Religious & Cultural Anchors: Bhakti: Advaita (Shankara), Vishishtadvaita (Ramanuja). Sufi : Chisti (Auliya), Suhrawardi. Literature : Amir Khusrau (Sultanate), Tulsidas/Abul Fazl (Mughal), Krishnadeva Raya (Telugu).
  • Mughal Revenue & Ranks: Zabti/Dahshala: Measured revenue. Zat : Personal rank. Sawar : Horseman count. Polaj : Best land. Banjar : Worst land.
  • Foreign Travelers Recap: Ibn Battuta (MBT - Sultanate). Nicolo Conti/Abdur Razzaq (Vijayanagara). Megasthenes (Maurya - recap). Fa-Hien/Hiuen Tsang (Ancient - recap).

##### DAY 36: UPSC History: Advent of Europeans & Carnatic Wars
  • Sequence of Arrival: 1. Portuguese (Vasco da Gama 1498). 2. Dutch (1602). 3. English (1608 - Captain Hawkins). 4. Danes (1616). 5. French (1664 - Colbert). Remember : P-D-E-D-F.
  • Portuguese in India: Almeida (Blue Water Policy). Albuquerque (Real founder, captured Goa 1510). They introduced Tobacco and Pineapple in India. Their power declined due to religious intolerance and competition.
  • The Carnatic Wars (British vs French): 1. First (1746-48): Battle of St. Thome, Treaty of Aix-la-Chapelle. 2. Second (1749-54): Battle of Ambur, Treaty of Pondicherry. 3. Third (1758-63): Battle of Wandiwash (1760 - French defeated), Treaty of Paris.
  • Dupleix & Robert Clive: Dupleix (French Governor): Pioneered the idea of interfering in Indian politics. Robert Clive (British): The mastermind behind British victory and the use of the "Sepoy" army.
  • Causes of British Success: Strong Naval power. Financial stability of the EIC (Private company, whereas French State-owned). Industrial Revolution in Britain. Efficient military leadership.

##### DAY 37: UPSC History: Battle of Plassey & Buxar (Conquest of Bengal)
  • Battle of Plassey (June 23, 1757): Siraj-ud-Daulah (Nawab of Bengal), whereas Robert Clive. Causes : Misuse of Dastaks (trade permits), Fortification of Calcutta. Outcome : Mir Jafar betrayed the Nawab. British became the "Kingmakers".
  • Battle of Buxar (1764): Mir Qasim (Bengal) + Shuja-ud-Daulah (Awadh) + Shah Alam II (Mughal), whereas Hector Munro. Decisive military victory. Proved British military superiority over combined Indian forces.
  • Treaty of Allahabad (1765): Ended Buxar. EIC got the "Diwani Rights" (Right to collect revenue) of Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa. This gave them massive financial resources without any administrative responsibility.
  • Dual Government in Bengal (1765–1772): System by Robert Clive. Diwani (Revenue) with EIC, Nizamat (Admin) with Nawab. Resulted in massive corruption, famine (1770), and collapse of Bengal's economy.
  • Key Terms: Dastak: Trade permit. Diwani : Revenue collection. Nizamat : Police and judicial functions.

##### DAY 38: UPSC History: Mysore, Maratha & Sikh Wars
  • Anglo-Mysore Wars (1767–1799): 1. 1st: Hyder Ali won. 2. 2nd: Hyder died, Treaty of Mangalore. 3. 3rd: Tipu defeated, Treaty of Seringapatam (lost half territory). 4. 4th (1799): Tipu died at Seringapatam. Wellesley ended Mysore's independence.
  • Anglo-Maratha Wars (1775–1818): 1. 1st: Treaty of Salbai (20 years of peace). 2. 2nd: Scindia/Holkar defeated, Treaty of Bassein (Subsidiary Alliance). 3. 3rd (1817-18): Peshwaship abolished. Marathas became pensioned off.
  • Anglo-Sikh Wars (1845–1849): Following Ranjit Singh's death, internal chaos led to wars. 2nd War (1849): Dalhousie annexed Punjab. Koh-i-Noor diamond taken to Britain.
  • Subsidiary Alliance (Wellesley): Indian states had to: 1. Keep a British army at their cost. 2. Post a British Resident. 3. Not employ other Europeans. 1st state: Hyderabad (1798).
  • Doctrine of Lapse (Dalhousie): If a ruler of a protected state died without a natural heir, the state would be annexed. Used to annex Satara, Jhansi, Nagpur. This was a major cause of the 1857 Revolt.

##### DAY 39: UPSC History: British Land Revenue Systems
  • Permanent Settlement (Zamindari): 1793: Lord Cornwallis (Bengal, Bihar, Odisha). Zamindars became hereditary owners. "Sunset Law" : Revenue had to be paid by a fixed date or land was auctioned. Benefit : Steady income for EIC. Loss : No incentive for land improvement, peasant suffering.
  • Ryotwari System: 1820: Thomas Munro and Alexander Reed (Madras, Bombay, Assam). Revenue fixed directly with the "Ryot" (peasant). No middlemen (Zamindars). Revenue was high and could be revised every 20-30 years.
  • Mahalwari System: 1822: Holt Mackenzie and Robert Merttins Bird (North-West Province, Punjab, Central India). Unit was "Mahal" (Village). Village community was collectively responsible for payment. Often mediated by the Lambardar (village head).
  • Commercialization of Agriculture: Shift from food crops (Paddy, Wheat) to cash crops (Indigo, Cotton, Jute, Tea). Led to the "De-industrialization" of India and frequent famines.
  • Drain of Wealth Theory: Propounded by Dadabhai Naoroji ("Poverty and Un-British Rule in India"). Explained how India's wealth was being transferred to Britain without any return.

##### DAY 40: UPSC History: Educational & Social Reforms
  • Raja Ram Mohan Roy & Brahmo Samaj: "Father of Modern India". Founded Brahmo Sabha (1828). Opposed Sati (Abolished 1829 by Bentinck), Idol worship, and Caste system. Published "Sambad Kaumudi" (Bengali).
  • Educational Reforms: 1. Charter Act 1813 (1 lakh for education). 2. Macaulay's Minute (1835 - "English is better than vernacular"). 3. Wood's Despatch (1854 - "Magna Carta of English Education"). Focus on primary to university education.
  • Women's Reformers: Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar: Championed Widow Remarriage (Act 1856). Jyotiba Phule : Founded Satyashodhak Samaj, worked for lower castes and women ("Gulamgiri"). Pandita Ramabai : Arya Mahila Samaj.
  • Hindu Revivalist/Reformist: Arya Samaj (Dayanand Saraswati): "Go back to Vedas", Shuddhi movement. Ramakrishna Mission (Swami Vivekananda): Practical Vedanta, Parliament of Religions (1893).
  • Socio-Religious Acts: Sati Abolition (1829), Widow Remarriage Act (1856), Age of Consent Act (1891), Sharda Act (1929 - Child marriage).

##### DAY 41: UPSC History: Tribal & Peasant Revolts (Pre-1857)
  • Sanyasi & Fakir Rebellion (1763–1800): Bengal. Sanyasis and Fakirs against British restrictions and high taxes. Immortalized in Bankim Chandra's "Anandamath" (Vande Mataram source).
  • Santhal Rebellion (1855–56): Leaders: Sidhu and Kanhu. Region : Rajmahal Hills (Daman-i-koh). Against Zamindars and "Dikus" (outsiders). It was the most massive tribal uprising before 1857.
  • Munda Rebellion (Ulgulan): Leader: Birsa Munda (1899-1900 - Note: Post-1857 but crucial). Against "Khuntkatti" (collective land) destruction by British. Birsa was called "Dharti Aba".
  • Indigo Revolt (1859–60): Bengal. Peasants refused to grow Indigo under exploitative contracts. Leaders : Digambar and Bishnu Biswas. Described in "Nil Darpan" by Dinabandhu Mitra.
  • Other Key Revolts: Paika Rebellion (Odisha 1817 - Bakshi Jagabandhu). Ramosi Revolt (Chittur Singh). Kol Mutiny (Buddho Bhagat). Farazi Movement (Haji Shariatullah).

##### DAY 42: UPSC History: The Revolt of 1857 — The First War
  • Causes of the Revolt: Political: Doctrine of Lapse, annexation of Awadh. Economic : Heavy taxation, destruction of handicrafts. Social/Religious: Interference in customs, Sati abolition, conversion fears. Military : Greased cartridges (immediate cause), discrimination against Indian sepoys.
  • Centers & Leaders: Delhi: Bahadur Shah Zafar (Nominal), Bakht Khan (Real). Kanpur : Nana Saheb, Tantia Tope. Lucknow : Begum Hazrat Mahal. Jhansi : Rani Lakshmibai. Bareilly : Khan Bahadur Khan. Arrah (Bihar): Kunwar Singh.
  • Suppression of the Revolt: Delhi: John Nicholson. Lucknow : Colin Campbell. Jhansi : Hugh Rose (who called Rani the "only man among the rebels").
  • Causes of Failure: Localized nature (South and West remained quiet). Lack of unified leadership and modern weapons. Support of some Indian rulers (Nizam, Scindia) to the British. Middle class remained neutral.
  • Impact of the Revolt: Government of India Act 1858: End of Company rule, India under British Crown. Secretary of State and Viceroy appointed. End of "Doctrine of Lapse". "Divide and Rule" policy intensified.
