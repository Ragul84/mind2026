**THE SAGA OF THE DELHI SULTANATE (1206–1526)**

**Act 1: The Slave Who Built an Empire (Mamluk Dynasty – 1206–1290)**

*Once upon a time in 1206, a former slave named Qutb-ud-din Aibak became the first Sultan of Delhi. He wasn't born royal—he earned it. He built the *Qutb Minar* as a victory tower (though he didn't finish it—his successor Iltutmish did). The dynasty is called "Slave" because its first three rulers were slaves who became generals.*

- **KEY POINT 1:** Iltutmish (1211-1236) saved the Sultanate from Mongol attacks and introduced the *silver tanka* (the first unified currency in India).
- **KEY POINT 2:** Razia Sultana (1236-1240) – only female ruler of the Sultanate. She failed because nobles (the "Forty") opposed a woman ruling.

**Act 2: The Khilji Revolution (1290–1320)**

*Enter Alauddin Khilji, the ruthless reformer. He wanted to control everything — prices, markets, and rebellions. He even banned alcohol and secret parties! But his masterstroke was the **Market Control Policy**: fixed prices for grain, cloth, and horses. He also stopped Mongol invasions 12 times.*

- **KEY CONCEPT:** He introduced *daghi* (branding of horses) and *huliya* (soldier's description) to stop corruption in the army.

**Act 3: The Tughlaq Madness (1320–1414)**

*Meet Muhammad bin Tughlaq – the genius who made terrible decisions. He decided to shift the capital from Delhi to Daulatabad (why? Nobody knows!). He also introduced token currency (copper coins worth silver) – and people started making fake coins at home. The plan failed disastrously.*

- **EXAM POINT:** His four failures: Capital shift, Token currency, Tax increase in Doab, Khurasan invasion plan.

**Act 4: The Sayyid and Lodi Sunset (1414–1526)**

*Weak rulers. The Lodi dynasty ended when Babur defeated Ibrahim Lodi at the First Battle of Panipat (1526). The Sultanate collapsed. But it left behind an Indo-Islamic culture.*
