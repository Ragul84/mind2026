### 📖 Story: The Rise and Fall of the Delhi Sultanate

Once upon a time (1206 CE), a slave-general named **Qutb-ud-din Aibak** became the first Sultan of Delhi after the Ghurid Empire collapsed. He built the **Qutb Minar** as a victory tower. But his reign was short—he died in a polo accident.

Then came the **Khiljis** (1290–1320). **Alauddin Khilji** was a fierce ruler. He conquered Gujarat, Ranthambore, and even fought off Mongol invasions (he built a new capital at Siri). He controlled prices and paid soldiers cheaply—clever, but harsh.

Next, the **Tughlaqs** (1320–1414). **Muhammad bin Tughlaq** was a genius with crazy ideas: he moved the capital from Delhi to Daulatabad (forced march, many died), and introduced token currency (copper coins worth silver—failed). His cousin **Firuz Shah Tughlaq** was kinder—built canals, hospitals, and gardens.

Then the **Sayyids** and **Lodis** (1414–1526). The last Lodi ruler, **Ibrahim Lodi**, lost the **First Battle of Panipat (1526)** to Babur, ending the Sultanate.

### 📝 Important Exam Points

- **Five Dynasties**: Slave (1206–1290), Khilji (1290–1320), Tughlaq (1320–1414), Sayyid (1414–1451), Lodi (1451–1526).  
- **Key Rulers**: Qutb-ud-din Aibak (built Qutb Minar), Iltutmish (introduced silver tanka coin), Alauddin Khilji (market control, Mongol defense), Muhammad bin Tughlaq (failed experiments), Firuz Shah (public works), Ibrahim Lodi (last Sultan).  
- **Battles**: First Battle of Tarain (1192, Prithviraj Chauhan lost to Ghurids), Battle of Panipat (1526, Babur vs Lodi).  
- **Administration**: Iqta system (land grants), Diwan-i-Risalat (army), Diwan-i-Kohi (agriculture under Tughlaqs).
