### Q: Explain photosynthesis

**Photosynthesis** is the process by which green plants, algae, and some bacteria convert sunlight into chemical energy. Simply put, they make their own food using **carbon dioxide**, **water**, and **light energy**, with **chlorophyll** acting as the catalyst. The end products are **carbohydrates** (like glucose) and **oxygen**, which is released into the air.

The overall chemical equation looks like this:

**6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂**

(Notice: the oxygen released comes from water, not from carbon dioxide. This was proved by Ruben and Kamen using radioisotopes, and earlier inferred by Cornelius van Niel from his studies on bacteria.)

---

### The two main phases

1) **Light Reaction** (also called the photochemical phase)  
   - Takes place in the **thylakoid membranes** of chloroplasts.  
   - **Chlorophyll** absorbs light energy, which splits water molecules (**photolysis**). This produces **electrons, protons, and oxygen**.  
   - Two pigment systems work: **Photosystem I** (reaction centre **P700**) and **Photosystem II** (reaction centre **P680**).  
   - Energy from electrons is used to make **ATP** and **NADPH** through cyclic and non-cyclic **photophosphorylation**.  
   - Oxygen is released as a byproduct.

2) **Dark Reaction** (also called the biosynthetic phase or Calvin cycle)  
   - Does not need light directly, but uses the **ATP** and **NADPH** made in the light reaction.  
   - **Carbon dioxide** is reduced to form **carbohydrates**.  
   - In C₃ plants, the first stable compound is a 3-carbon molecule (PGA), and the CO₂ acceptor is **RuBP** (ribulose bisphosphate).  
   - This phase takes place in the **stroma** of the chloroplast.

---

### Why it matters

Photosynthesis is the most important **anabolic** process on Earth. It is the foundation of the food chain, produces the oxygen we breathe, and is the only natural way to convert solar energy into a form that living organisms can use. For competitive exams, remember the key experiments (Engelmann's action spectrum using Cladophora, van Niel's bacterial studies, Ruben and Kamen's isotope work) and the difference between the two photosystems and the two phases.

### Q: What are the Fundamental Rights in the Indian Constitution?

The **Fundamental Rights** are enshrined in **Part III** of the Indian Constitution, covering **Articles 12 to 35**. Originally, there were **seven** rights, but now only **six** remain (the Right to Property was removed from Part III by the **44th Amendment, 1978** and made a legal right under Article 300A). These rights were inspired by the **US Constitution** and are judicially enforceable — you can directly approach the Supreme Court under **Article 32** (Right to Constitutional Remedies) if any fundamental right is violated. (Ref: SAMACHEER Class 10 social-science)

Here are the six Fundamental Rights as they stand today:

1) **Right to Equality (Articles 14–18)**  
   - **Article 14**: Equality before law and equal protection of laws (rule of law).  
   - **Article 15**: No discrimination on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex, or place of birth.  
   - **Article 16**: Equality of opportunity in public employment.  
   - **Article 17**: Abolition of untouchability.  
   - **Article 18**: Abolition of titles (except military and academic).  

2) **Right to Freedom (Articles 19–22)**  
   - **Article 19**: Six freedoms — speech, assembly, association, movement, residence, profession (with reasonable restrictions).  
   - **Article 20**: Protection in respect of conviction for offences (ex post facto law, double jeopardy, self-incrimination).  
   - **Article 21**: Protection of life and personal liberty.  
   - **Article 21A**: Right to education for children aged **6–14 years** (added by 86th Amendment, 2002; enforced through RTE Act, 2009).  
   - **Article 22**: Protection against arrest and detention in certain cases.

3) **Right against Exploitation (Articles 23–24)**  
   - **Article 23**: Prohibition of trafficking, forced labour, and beggary.  
   - **Article 24**: Prohibition of employment of children below **14 years** in factories, mines, etc.

4) **Right to Freedom of Religion (Articles 25–28)**  
   - **Article 25**: Freedom of conscience and free profession, practice, and propagation of religion.  
   - **Article 26**: Freedom to manage religious affairs.  
   - **Article 27**: No compulsion to pay taxes for promotion of any religion.  
   - **Article 28**: No religious instruction in state-run educational institutions.

5) **Cultural and Educational Rights (Articles 29–30)**  
   - **Article 29**: Protection of language, script, and culture of minorities.  
   - **Article 30**: Right of minorities to establish and administer educational institutions.

6) **Right to Constitutional Remedies (Article 32)**  
   - The "heart and soul" of the Constitution (Dr. Ambedkar).  
   - You can move the **Supreme Court** directly for enforcement of any fundamental right.  
   - The Court can issue **five kinds of writs**: Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, and Quo Warranto.  
   - **High Courts** also have writ jurisdiction under **Article 226**, but Article 32 is specifically for the Supreme Court.

**Important points for exams:**  
- Fundamental Rights are **not absolute**; the state can impose **reasonable restrictions** in the interest of public order, morality, etc.  
- They are available to **citizens** only in some cases (e.g., Articles 15, 16, 19, 29, 30) and to **all persons** in others (e.g., Articles 14, 21, 25).  
- **Part III** is often called the **Magna Carta of India** because it guarantees basic civil liberties enforceable by courts.  
- The **42nd Amendment (1976)** added **Fundamental Duties** in **Part IVA (Article 51A)** — these are not rights but moral obligations.

### Q: Explain the powers and functions of the Governor of a State

The Governor is the constitutional head of a State, appointed by the **President** and holding office during the **pleasure of the President** (usually five years). (Ref: SAMACHEER Class 10) The Governor's powers and functions are broadly executive and legislative.

### Executive Powers

- The Governor is the **executive head** of the State Government. All executive actions are taken in his name, and his powers extend to matters in the **State List** (Article 154). (Ref: SAMACHEER Class 12)
- He appoints the leader of the majority party in the Legislative Assembly as the **Chief Minister** and other ministers on the CM's advice. The Council of Ministers holds office during the pleasure of the Chief Minister, and the Governor acts on their aid and advice (Article 163). (Ref: SAMACHEER Class 12 & Class 10)
- He appoints the **Advocate General**, the **Chairman and Members of the State Public Service Commission**, and determines appointments, postings, and promotions of judges of subordinate courts. (Ref: SAMACHEER Class 12)
- If the constitutional machinery of the State breaks down, the Governor can recommend **President's Rule** under **Article 356**. During such rule, the Governor administers the State on behalf of the President. (Ref: SAMACHEER Class 12)

### Legislative Powers

- The Governor is a part of the **State Legislature** (Article 168). (Ref: SAMACHEER Class 12)
- He summons, prorogues, and dissolves the **Legislative Assembly**. He addresses the legislature at the start of the first session after each general election. (Ref: SAMACHEER Class 12 & Class 10)
- No Bill can become law without the Governor's **assent**. He may give assent, withhold it, return the Bill for reconsideration (but if the legislature passes it again, he must give assent), or **reserve the Bill for the President's consideration**. (Ref: SAMACHEER Class 12)
- Under **Article 213**, the Governor can promulgate **ordinances** when the legislature is not in session. The ordinance must be approved by the legislature within six weeks of reassembly. (Ref: SAMACHEER Class 12)

### Key Points for Exams

- The Governor is appointed by the **President**, not elected. (Ref: SAMACHEER Class 10)
- **Article 154** vests executive power in the Governor. (Ref: SAMACHEER Class 10)
- The Governor's discretionary powers (e.g., appointing CM when no clear majority) are not directly mentioned in these passages, but remember that the "aid and advice" rule has exceptions under the Constitution.
- The Governor can also be transferred by the President and can resign by addressing the President. (Ref: SAMACHEER Class 10)

### Q: What were the main causes of the Revolt of 1857?

The Revolt of 1857 was not a sudden explosion. It was the result of long-term grievances that had been building up for decades across political, economic, social, military, and religious spheres. Here is a clear breakdown of the main causes.

### 1. Political Causes
The British policy of aggressive expansion had alienated almost every Indian ruler and their subjects.
- **Doctrine of Lapse:** Introduced by Lord Dalhousie, this policy allowed the British to annex any princely state where the ruler died without a natural heir. States like **Satara, Jhansi, Nagpur, and Sambalpur** were annexed, directly angering the displaced rulers and their people. (Ref: SAMACHEER Class 8 social-science)
- **Subsidiary Alliance:** This system forced Indian rulers to keep British troops in their states and pay for their maintenance, effectively making them puppets. It drained the treasuries of states like Awadh and Hyderabad.
- **Annexation of Awadh (Oudh):** This was a major political blunder. Awadh was annexed in 1856 on the flimsy ground of "misgovernment," even though the Nawab had been a loyal ally. The annexation of Awadh proved that even the most grovelling loyalty could not satisfy British greed for territories. (Ref: SAMACHEER Class 8 social-science) This displaced the taluqdars (landlords), soldiers, and artisans who depended on the Nawab's court.

### 2. Economic Causes
This was the most important cause, as it hurt **all sections of society**. (Ref: SAMACHEER Class 8 social-science)
- **Peasants:** They suffered due to **high revenue demands** and a very **strict revenue collection policy**. The British introduced a new land revenue system that was far more exploitative than earlier ones. (Ref: SAMACHEER Class 8 social-science)
- **Artisans and Craftsmen:** British manufactured goods flooded the Indian market, destroying traditional industries like textiles. Indian weavers and handicraft workers lost their livelihoods.
- **Zamindars and Taluqdars:** Even loyal landlords were dispossessed under new revenue settlements, as the British auctioned off their lands for non-payment of revenue.

### 3. Social and Religious Causes
The British interference in Indian traditions created widespread fear and resentment.
- **Missionary Activities:** The conversion activities of Christian missionaries were looked upon with suspicion and fear. (Ref: SAMACHEER Class 8 social-science)
- **Social Reforms:** While well-intentioned, the **abolition of practices like sati, female infanticide, support to widow remarriage, and women's education** were seen by many as interference in their Indian culture by the Europeans. (Ref: SAMACHEER Class 8 social-science) It created a fear that the British wanted to forcibly convert Indians to Christianity.
- **Tax on Religious Places:** The British imposed taxes on mosques and temples, further fueling religious anger.

### 4. Military Causes
The Indian sepoys, who formed the backbone of the British army, were deeply dissatisfied.
- **Discrimination:** Indian sepoys were treated as inferior beings and with contempt by their British officers. They were paid much less than British soldiers. (Ref: SAMACHEER Class 8 social-science)
- **No Promotions:** All avenues of promotion were closed to them, as all higher army posts were reserved for the British. (Ref: SAMACHEER Class 8 social-science)
- **General Service Act:** A new law forced sepoys to serve overseas, which was against the religious beliefs of many Hindus who would lose their caste.

### 5. The Immediate Cause: The Greased Cartridges
The spark that ignited the fire was the introduction of the new **Enfield Rifle**.
- The cartridge of this rifle had to be bitten off with the mouth before loading.
- A rumour spread that the cartridges were greased with the fat of **pigs and cows**.
- This was a deliberate attempt to destroy the religion of both **Hindus** (who revere the cow) and **Muslims** (who hate the pig). (Ref: SAMACHEER Class 8 social-science)
- The sepoys refused to use the cartridges, leading to the outbreak of the revolt.

### The Outbreak
On **29 March 1857**, at Barrackpur (near Kolkata), **Mangal Pandey**, a young sepoy from the Bengal Regiment, refused to use the greased cartridge and shot down his sergeant. He was arrested, tried, and executed. When this news spread, many sepoys revolted. (Ref: SAMACHEER Class 8 social-science) This marked the beginning of the widespread rebellion.

### Q: நமது இதயத்தின் அமைப்பு மற்றும் செயல்பாடு பற்றி விளக்குக

நிச்சயமாக! இதயம் நமது உடலில் மிக முக்கியமான உறுப்பு. அதன் **அமைப்பு** (structure) மற்றும் **செயல்பாடு** (function) பற்றி எளிமையாகப் பார்க்கலாம்.

### அமைப்பு (Structure)

நமது இதயம் நான்கு **அறைகள்** (chambers) கொண்டது. இது ஒரு வலுவான தசைப் பை போன்றது.

1) **மேல் இரு அறைகள்: இடது மேலறை (Left Atrium) & வலது மேலறை (Right Atrium)** – இவை இரத்தத்தைப் பெறும் அறைகள். வலது மேலறை உடல் முழுவதிலிருந்து திரும்பி வரும் **ஆக்சிஜன் குறைந்த** இரத்தத்தையும், இடது மேலறை நுரையீரலிலிருந்து வரும் **ஆக்சிஜன் நிறைந்த** இரத்தத்தையும் பெறுகிறது.

2) **கீழ் இரு அறைகள்: இடது கீழறை (Left Ventricle) & வலது கீழறை (Right Ventricle)** – இவை இரத்தத்தை வெளியேற்றும் அறைகள். இடது கீழறை மிகவும் வலிமையானது, ஏனெனில் அது ஆக்சிஜன் நிறைந்த இரத்தத்தை உடல் முழுவதற்கும் பம்ப் செய்கிறது. வலது கீழறை ஆக்சிஜன் குறைந்த இரத்தத்தை நுரையீரலுக்கு அனுப்புகிறது.

3) **இதய வால்வுகள் (Heart Valves)** – இரத்தம் எப்போதும் ஒரே திசையில் மட்டும் பாய்வதை உறுதி செய்கின்றன. பின் பாய்வதைத் தடுக்கின்றன.

4) **பிரிசுவர் (Septum)** – இதயத்தின் இடது மற்றும் வலது பக்கங்களைப் பிரிக்கும் சுவர். இது ஆக்சிஜன் நிறைந்த இரத்தமும் ஆக்சிஜன் குறைந்த இரத்தமும் கலக்காமல் தடுக்கிறது.

### செயல்பாடு (Function)

இதயம் ஒரு **பம்ப்** போல வேலை செய்கிறது. அதன் வேலை **இரத்தச் சுழற்சி** (blood circulation).

*   **செயல்பாட்டு பொறிமுறை:** இதயத்தின் தசைகள் தாளமாக சுருங்கி (contraction / systole) விரிவடைகின்றன (relaxation / diastole). இதை 'இதயத் துடிப்பு' (heartbeat) என்கிறோம். **SA node** (Sinoatrial node) எனும் இயற்கையான மின் கட்டுப்பாட்டு மையமே இந்த தாளத்தைத் தொடங்கி வைக்கிறது.

*   **இரத்தச் சுழற்சியின் பாதை:**
    1) **நுரையீரல் சுழற்சி (Pulmonary Circulation):** வலது கீழறை ஆக்சிஜன் குறைந்த இரத்தத்தை **நுரையீரல் தமனி** (Pulmonary artery) வழியாக நுரையீரலுக்கு அனுப்புகிறது. அங்கு ஆக்சிஜன் சேர்ந்த பின், **நுரையீரல் சிரை** (Pulmonary vein) வழியாக இடது மேலறைக்குத் திரும்புகிறது.
    2) **உடல் சுழற்சி (Systemic Circulation):** இடது கீழறை ஆக்சிஜன் நிறைந்த இரத்தத்தை **பெருந்தமனி** (Aorta) வழியாக உடல் முழுவதற்கும் பம்ப் செய்கிறது. திசுக்கள் ஆக்சிஜனைப் பயன்படுத்திய பின், இரத்தம் **மேல் மற்றும் கீழ் பெருஞ்சிரைகள்** (Superior & Inferior Vena Cava) வழியாக வலது மேலறைக்குத் திரும்புகிறது.
