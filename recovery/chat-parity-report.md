### Q: How is the President of India elected?

The President of India is elected indirectly through an **Electoral College**, not directly by the common people. The election is conducted using the **system of proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote**, and voting is by **secret ballot**. (Ref: SAMACHEER Class 12 political-science)

Here is the step-by-step breakdown:

**1. Who constitutes the Electoral College?**
The Electoral College consists only of **elected members** from:
- Both Houses of Parliament (**Lok Sabha** and **Rajya Sabha**)
- The **Legislative Assemblies** (Vidhan Sabhas) of all States
- The Legislative Assemblies of the **Union Territories of Delhi and Puducherry**

**Important:** The following are **NOT** part of the Electoral College:
- Nominated members of either House of Parliament (e.g., the 12 nominated Rajya Sabha members)
- Nominated members of State Legislative Assemblies
- Members of **Legislative Councils** (Vidhan Parishads) in bicameral states
- Nominated members of the UTs of Delhi and Puducherry

This is done to ensure that only **directly elected representatives** vote for the President, maintaining democratic legitimacy. (Ref: NCERT Class 8 social-science)

**2. Qualifications (Article 58)**
To be eligible, a candidate must:
- Be a **citizen of India**
- Have completed **35 years of age**
- Be qualified for election as a member of the **Lok Sabha**
- **Not** hold any **office of profit** under the Union, State, or local government
- Be proposed by at least **50 electors** and seconded by another **50 electors** from the Electoral College (Ref: SAMACHEER Class 12 political-science)

**3. The Voting System: Single Transferable Vote**
This system ensures the winner has the support of an absolute majority. Voters rank candidates in order of preference (1, 2, 3...). If no candidate gets the required quota of first-preference votes, the last-placed candidate is eliminated, and their votes are transferred to the voters' second preferences. This process continues until one candidate crosses the quota.

**4. Security Deposit**
Every candidate must make a security deposit of **₹15,000** with the RBI. This deposit is forfeited if the candidate fails to secure **1/6th of the votes polled**. (Ref: SAMACHEER Class 12 political-science)

**5. Term and Oath**
The President is elected for a term of **five years** and is eligible for re-election. After election, the President takes an oath of office before the **Chief Justice of India**. (Ref: SAMACHEER Class 10 social-science)

**Why this method?**
Since the President is the **symbol of unity, integrity, and solidarity of the nation**, this indirect method ensures the support of both the central and state governments, giving a greater say to the more populous states. (Ref: NCERT Class 8 social-science)

### Q: Explain the process of photosynthesis as per Samacheer Kalvi science

**Photosynthesis** is a photochemical oxidation-reduction process where plants convert solar energy into chemical energy. It is the most important anabolic process on Earth.

The overall equation is:
**6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂** (in the presence of sunlight and chlorophyll)

---

### Two major phases

**1) Light reaction (Photochemical phase)**
- Takes place in the thylakoid membranes of chloroplasts.
- **Chlorophyll** traps sunlight energy.
- Two pigment systems work: **Photosystem I (PS I)** with reaction centre **P700** and **Photosystem II (PS II)** with reaction centre **P680**.
- **Photolysis** of water occurs – water splits into electrons, protons, and oxygen. The oxygen is released as a by-product.
- Through **cyclic and non-cyclic photophosphorylation**, energy (ATP) and reducing power (NADPH) are generated.

**2) Dark reaction (Biosynthetic phase / Calvin cycle)**
- Does not require light directly; uses the ATP and NADPH produced in the light reaction.
- **Carbon dioxide is reduced** to form carbohydrates.
- In **C₃ plants**, the first stable product is a 3-carbon compound (PGA), and the cycle begins with **RuBP** (Ribulose bisphosphate).
- The final product is **glucose**, a simple carbohydrate, which is later converted into **starch** for storage.

---

### Key points to remember for exams
- **Raw materials**: CO₂, H₂O, sunlight, chlorophyll.
- **Site**: Chloroplast (specifically grana for light reaction, stroma for dark reaction).
- **Light reaction oxidises water, dark reaction reduces CO₂**.
- **Glucose** is the immediate product; **starch** is the storage form.
- The process was discovered over time by many scientists; in India, **Rustom Hormusji Dastur** studied the effects of water, temperature, and light colour on photosynthesis.

This explanation covers the core process from the basic level (raw materials and starch storage) to the advanced level (photophosphorylation, PS I/PS II, Calvin cycle) – exactly the way the Kalvi science curriculum builds it up.

**முகவுரை** என்றால் அரசியலமைப்பின் அறிமுகம் அல்லது முன்னுரை. இது அரசியலமைப்பின் **அடிப்படைக் கொள்கைகள், நோக்கங்கள் மற்றும் இலட்சியங்களை** உள்ளடக்கியது. இதை "அரசியலமைப்பின் திறவுகோல்" (Key to the Constitution) என்று அழைப்பார்கள். (Ref: SAMACHEER Class 10 social-science)

- **தொடக்கம்:** *"இந்திய மக்களாகிய நாம்"* என்ற சொற்களுடன் முகவுரை தொடங்குகிறது. இதிலிருந்து, **இந்திய மக்களே இந்திய அரசியலமைப்பின் ஆதாரம்** என்பது தெளிவாகிறது. (Ref: SAMACHEER Class 10 social-science)

- **சமயச்சார்பற்ற** (Secular) - 42வது திருத்தத்தில் சேர்க்கப்பட்டது

### Q: Solve this MCQ: Article 44 of the Indian Constitution deals with? a) Uniform Civil Code b) Village Panchayats c) Official Language d) Emergency Powers

Answer: **a) Uniform Civil Code**

Article 44 is a **Directive Principle of State Policy** under Part IV of the Constitution. It directs the State to endeavour to secure a **Uniform Civil Code** for all citizens throughout the territory of India. The idea is to replace personal laws based on religion with a common set of laws governing marriage, divorce, inheritance, adoption, etc.

Why the other options are wrong:
- **b) Village Panchayats** – Article 40 (Directive Principle) deals with organising village panchayats.
- **c) Official Language** – Articles 343–351 in Part XVII deal with official language.
- **d) Emergency Powers** – Articles 352, 356, 360 in Part XVIII deal with emergency provisions (national, state, financial).

So for the exam, remember: **Article 44 = Uniform Civil Code** (a DPSP, not a Fundamental Right).
